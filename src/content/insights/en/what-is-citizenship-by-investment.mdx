Citizenship by investment is a legal route, established under the law of certain countries, that allows foreign investors to obtain citizenship by meeting the requirements of an official government programme. It is not a shortcut and not a loophole — it is a defined legislative framework, applied through a formal review procedure.

## Why governments create these programmes

States design citizenship-by-investment programmes to attract international capital that supports national development — funding infrastructure, job creation, education and other public projects. In exchange, qualifying investors who meet the programme's legal requirements are granted citizenship.

Each programme is governed by its own national legislation, with its own investment options, due diligence process, and review timeline. No two programmes are identical, which is precisely why comparing them requires care rather than assumption.

## What the process typically involves

While the specifics vary by country, most programmes follow a similar sequence:

- an initial assessment of eligibility
- selection of an investment option permitted under the programme
- preparation and submission of a complete application
- a due diligence review conducted by government authorities
- a formal decision, followed by the completion of citizenship formalities

## What it is not

Citizenship by investment is not a guarantee, and it is not a transaction completed "in weeks" regardless of what promotional language elsewhere might suggest. The final decision always rests with the competent state authorities, and the review considers the full profile of the applicant — not investment alone.

## Why this matters before you choose a programme

Because each programme differs in its requirements, eligible family composition, and long-term implications, the right starting point is not a list of countries — it is a clear understanding of your own goals. That is the basis on which a suitable recommendation can be made.
